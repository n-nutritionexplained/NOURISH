THE BODY · INVESTIGATION 02

Metabolism: the
word doing
the most
lying.

Everyone blames theirs.

Almost nobody's using the word the way scientists actually do.

01 THE ACCUSATION

"My metabolism is slow" is one of the most common explanations people give for their weight, their energy levels, or their relationship with food.

It's usually said with a specific mental image attached: a dial somewhere inside you, permanently set too low, quietly working against everything you do.

Let's find out what that dial actually is — and whether it's really set the way people assume.

02 START HERE

What metabolism
actually is.

Metabolism isn't a single dial or a single number. It's the sum of every chemical reaction keeping you alive — building molecules, breaking them down, repairing tissue, firing neurons, keeping your heart beating whether you're thinking about it or not.

The part people usually mean when they say "metabolism" is your resting metabolic rate: the energy your body burns just existing. For most people, that's roughly 60–70% of total daily energy use — more than exercise, more than digestion, just from staying alive.

You are not doing anything wrong
by burning most of your calories at rest. That's what a body is supposed to do.

THE STUDY THAT REWROTE THE STORY

Your metabolism doesn't slow down in your thirties.

A 2021 study in Science tracked energy expenditure — adjusted for body size — in over 6,400 people aged 8 days to 95 years. It found four distinct life stages, and they don't line up with the milestones you'd expect.

Under 1

Metabolic rate rockets to about 50% above the adult rate — pound for pound, infants burn hotter than anyone.

1–20

A slow, steady decline down to adult levels — no sudden drop at puberty.

20–60

Remarkably stable — even through pregnancy, and with no meaningful difference between men and women of similar size.

60+

The real decline begins — decades later than the "it slows down in your 30s" story usually claims.

If your metabolism feels different at 35 than it did at 22, the honest culprit is far more likely to be muscle mass, activity level or sleep than a dial quietly turning down on schedule.

THREE MYTHS, RETIRED

The claims that outlived their own evidence.

THE MYTH

"Muscle burns way more calories than fat, so more muscle means a dramatically faster metabolism."

THE EVIDENCE

A kilogram of muscle burns roughly 13 kcal a day at rest, versus about 4.5 for a kilogram of fat — a real difference, but a modest one in absolute terms. Gaining a few kilograms of muscle adds meaningfully less to your daily burn than marketing implies.

THE MYTH

"Spicy food, green tea and cold water meaningfully boost your metabolism."

THE EVIDENCE

These effects are real but small — controlled studies on caffeine and capsaicin find modest, short-lived increases in energy expenditure, nowhere near enough to meaningfully affect weight on their own.

THE MYTH

"Eating too little will permanently damage your metabolism."

THE EVIDENCE

It's more specific than "damaged": significant, rapid weight loss does suppress resting metabolic rate, and that suppression can be surprisingly persistent — but it's an adaptation to the deficit, not permanent physical damage.

01

WHAT ADAPTATION ACTUALLY LOOKS LIKE

The most-cited weight-loss metabolism study you've never fully read.

Researchers followed 14 contestants from the reality show The Biggest Loser for six years after their dramatic, competition-driven weight loss.

Their resting metabolic rate had dropped by roughly 610 calories a day by the end of the show — and was still about 704 calories a day below expected, six years later, even after most of them regained the weight.

That's the real, well-documented shape of "metabolic adaptation": your body genuinely does become more energy-efficient after significant weight loss, and that shift can outlast the weight loss itself by years. It's a real biological response to a real deficit — evidence for taking rapid, extreme weight loss seriously, not evidence that dieting "breaks" you.

THE QUESTION I ACTUALLY CAME FOR

Okay, but can I
fix a slow metabolism?

So metabolism is mostly stable through most of adulthood, muscle helps only modestly, and "boosting" foods do almost nothing measurable. Which brings us to the question behind an entire supplement category.

Is there anything that actually raises my metabolic rate?

The pitch sounds precise.
The real levers are duller.

The metabolism-boosting pitch usually goes:

My metabolic rate feels too low.
Certain foods and supplements can raise it.
So the right product should fix the problem.
WHAT ACTUALLY MOVES THE NUMBER

The factors with real, measurable influence on resting metabolic rate are unglamorous: body size, muscle mass, age, and how much you move throughout the day — including fidgeting and incidental activity, not just formal exercise. None of them come in a bottle.

The question isn't really boost or no boost. It's which of the boring, unmarketable levers — muscle, movement, sleep — you're actually willing to pull.

CONTEXT MATTERS

So when is a metabolism concern actually medical?

01

An underactive or overactive thyroid, which genuinely does shift metabolic rate

02

PCOS and certain other hormonal conditions

03

Medications with a known effect on weight or appetite

04

A sudden, unexplained change in weight or energy — worth an actual conversation with a doctor

The less useful question is:

"How do I boost my metabolism?"

The more useful one is:

"Is there a real medical reason mine might be different, or am I chasing a dial that was never that broken?"

SO, WHAT DID I ACTUALLY LEARN?

Your metabolism is probably
faster than you expected.

I went looking for a dial. What I found instead was a remarkably stable system, four honest life stages that don't match the folklore, and one genuinely important exception — real metabolic adaptation after major weight loss — that gets flattened into a much vaguer, scarier idea than the evidence supports.

What I take from all of this is much simpler:
01

Your metabolism is probably not the villain you've been told it is.

02

The real levers — muscle, movement, sleep — are dull, unmarketable, and actually work.

03

Rapid, extreme weight loss has real metabolic consequences worth taking seriously, separate from vague "boosting" anxiety.

Not a dial someone set too low on you specifically.

Not something a tea or a pill meaningfully moves.

Just a stable, well-engineered system doing exactly what it evolved to do.

Your metabolism was never really the problem. The story about it usually was.

AND MAYBE THAT'S THE PART I WAS LOOKING FOR

Understanding gives us
better questions to ask.

I don't think the lesson of metabolism is that we should all stop caring about it entirely.

I think it's that the word got asked to explain far more than the biology actually supports — and most of that extra weight was marketing, not physiology.

"Why is my metabolism so slow?"

becomes

"Is there an actual medical reason to look into, or am I in the stable 20–60 range like most people?"

"Am I moving, sleeping and eating in a way the boring levers would actually respond to?"

"Is this product selling me physiology, or a feeling?"

Because a body is supposed to exist within a life, not spend that life being blamed for things a hormone panel would settle in one visit.

I came into this ready to be told my metabolism needed fixing. I left with a much calmer, much duller list of things that actually move the number — and permission to stop taking it personally.

Nourish yourself.
Your metabolism was doing its job the whole time.
